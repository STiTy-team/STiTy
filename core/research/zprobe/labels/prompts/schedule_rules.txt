Rules (all six are checked mechanically afterwards):
1. Nothing before it is heard: an item (k, text) may contain only information available from source words 1..k, and its "src" list names exactly the source words it translates (every number <= k). If a name, number, negation or tense comes later, wait for that word.
2. Nothing is revised: the items concatenated in order must form one complete, faithful, natural translation of the whole sentence; later items never rewrite or correct earlier ones.
3. Emit settled pieces at once: noun phrases, places, times and other content that later words cannot change are emitted as soon as they are heard - but only where the final sentence still reads naturally (plan the word order from the whole sentence first).
4. Pieces whose meaning depends on later words (verbs, negation, questions, tense) wait for the deciding word, but never past the end of their clause.
5. Deferral cap: no content is emitted more than {N} source words after the word that made it available.
6. Empty stretches must be justified: withholding something that could have been emitted is as bad as emitting unheard content.
