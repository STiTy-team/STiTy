- NUM–UNIT adjacency (moderate, conditional demotion):
  - If the token immediately before the marker is a numeral (digit-formatted number like 1,200 or a simple spelled-out numeral such as "one", "two", "three") and the token immediately after the marker is a measurement/unit word (examples to watch for: "million", "percent", "kilometers", "meter", "meters", "mile", "miles", "year", "years", "km", "m", "degree"), do NOT automatically boost this marker. Instead, apply a moderate downward ordering bias: rank such NUM–UNIT splits below other nearby markers that keep numeric+unit together or that separate stronger lexical clusters, because splitting a number from its unit often reduces single-side fidelity or creates contradictions. This is a soft ordering change (aim to move these markers toward mid-range relative ranks rather than to the extreme bottom) to correct measured over-trust in raw numeral adjacency.
  - Exception (preserve genuinely good numeral-adjacent boundaries): do not apply the demotion when one of the following is true: (a) the unit token is followed immediately by punctuation (comma, period) suggesting the unit ends the clause; (b) the left numeral is formatted with thousands separators or decimals (e.g., contains "," or "." in the token) indicating a clear numeric figure that translators commonly render alone; (c) the unit token is capitalized or otherwise looks like a standalone label. When an exception applies, keep the usual numeral-adjacent upward bias.
