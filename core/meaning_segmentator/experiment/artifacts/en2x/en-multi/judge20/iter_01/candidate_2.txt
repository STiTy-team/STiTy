[Core Principles]
- Does the continuation overturn, negate, or reverse the claim established by the stretch before this cut (e.g., via negation after auxiliaries, concessive subordinators, or contrastive parentheticals)? If so, the cut is harmful to faithful incremental translation.
- Does the continuation supply the predicate, object, or other argument that the heard fragment needs to form a coherent proposition in English SVO order? Cutting before such material causes missing or orphaned propositions.
- Does the continuation determine how a clause-initial temporal or locative adverbial attaches? If the adverbial's attachment is unresolved until later, cutting can lead to wrong temporal/locative relations.
- Does the continuation resolve the scope of unstable prefixes or scope-limiting particles (only, just) or approximate numerals? If scope is undecided, separate translation will misrepresent quantification or limitation.
- Does the continuation complete a phrasal verb or verb+particle sequence? These sequences are cohesive in English and splitting them damages verbal semantics.
- Keep a short (1–4 word) complement that immediately follows a verb or adjective together with that verb/adjective when the complement matches a surface pattern: a numeral (digits) optionally with unit tokens or parenthesized measure; 'in' or 'on' + a short date/time token; 'to' + verb (infinitive) or 'not to' + verb; or a direction marker (toward(s), toward, towards, north, south, east, west) possibly plus up to two words.
- Does the continuation provide essential post-nominal modification (relative clause or complement) that identifies or restricts a previously heard noun? Separating head and modifier risks mistranslation of referents.
- Does the continuation refine or constrain a numeral or quantifier inside a noun phrase? Numerals and quantifiers are internal to NPs in English; splitting them breaks quantity semantics.
- Does the continuation supply appositive identification, titles, possessive relations, or disambiguating material that assigns roles or identity to a previously heard name or noun? Cutting before such material can cause wrong attributions.

[Scoring Rules]
cohesion   the pieces were translated separately into Chinese, Japanese, German, Spanish, joined in order, and a reference-free quality estimator scored how faithfully that joined text renders the WHOLE source sentence.
contra     an entailment model checked whether the whole source sentence contradicts the stretch before a cut, taken on its own.
target = cohesion x (1 - contra)

- Treat cohesion and contra as the latent measured quantities above; contra is a graded probability and contributes only via the product. Do NOT convert contra into a binary flag or separate tier.
- Your numeric assignment is a RANK within the sentence: order the candidate cut positions by the target product and map that ordering onto distinct integers spanning the full 0–100 range.
- Do NOT attempt to compute the product arithmetically (do not instruct yourself to multiply or round); instead, use the product as the conceptual measure that defines the ordering you must reproduce.
- Do not add new scoring conditions; do not tier positions except by their product-derived ordering.

[Output Rules]

  Keep every marker and write an integer from 0 to 100 in place of the ?, so that <SEG:?>
  becomes for example <SEG:73>. Never output a bare number without the marker. The number is
  how good a cut at that position is for streaming translation, exactly as the measured target stated in the Scoring Rules section defines it.
- Your numbers are used ONLY to RANK the markers WITHIN THIS SENTENCE. A later step keeps the
  highest-scoring ones under the current latency budget and nothing else reads the numbers, so
  they are never compared across sentences. Spend your effort on the ORDER, not on hitting an
  absolute scale.
- Give every marker in a sentence a DIFFERENT number. Ties are broken by position (the earlier
  marker wins), which means a tie hands the decision to word order instead of to your judgment.
  Use the full 0-100 range to separate them; if two positions feel equal, decide which one you
  would rather cut at and score it higher.
- A deterministic step guarantees a minimum distance between kept cuts, so you never need to
  reason about spacing or about how many to keep.
- Do NOT add, remove, or move any marker. Do NOT change, correct, or reorder any words of the
  text. Keep exactly one space on both sides of every marker.
- Output the text with the filled markers and nothing else. No explanation, label, or commentary.
