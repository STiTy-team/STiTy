[Core Principles]
- Does the continuation overturn, negate, or reverse the claim established by the stretch before this cut (for example via negation realized after auxiliaries, concessive subordinators, or contrastive parentheticals)? If so, a cut there breaks faithful incremental rendering.
- Does the continuation supply the verb/predicate or the core complement (object, clausal complement, or predicative) that the already-heard subject needs to form a complete SVO proposition? Cutting before that leaves an orphaned or misleading assertion.
- Do not cut between a short (≤3-word) subject phrase (proper name, pronoun, or determiner+noun) and its immediately following verb or copula; separating them tends to produce orphan predicates or apparent contradictions.
- Are the next one to four words a short but essential predicate complement (numeric measure, brief time/place phrase, direction, negating or infinitival complement) that resolves the clause's truth value? Treat such short continuations as integral to the clause's cohesion.
- Does the continuation complete a phrasal verb or verb+particle sequence that together create an indivisible verbal meaning? Splitting these items damages the predicate's semantics.
- Does the continuation provide essential post-nominal modification (relative clause, complement, or appositive) that identifies or restricts a previously heard noun? Cutting before it risks wrong referent translation.
- Does the continuation resolve how a clause-initial temporal or locative adverbial attaches to the nearest verb phrase? If attachment is unresolved, separating the adverbial can alter the asserted time/place relation.
- Does the continuation determine the scope of scope-limiting particles, approximate numerals, conditional introducers, or modal framing? When scope is undecided, splitting will likely misrepresent quantification or conditionality.

[Scoring Rules]

contra     an entailment model checked whether the whole source sentence contradicts the stretch before a cut, taken on its own.
target = cohesion x (1 - contra)

- Treat cohesion and contra as the latent measured quantities above; contra is a graded probability and contributes only via the product. Do NOT convert contra into a binary flag or separate tier.
- Your numeric assignment is a RANK within the sentence: order the candidate cut positions by the target product and map that ordering onto distinct integers spanning the full 0–100 range.
- Do NOT attempt to compute the product arithmetically (do not instruct yourself to multiply or round); instead, use the product as the conceptual measure that defines the ordering you must reproduce.
- Do not add new scoring conditions; do not tier positions except by their product-derived ordering.
