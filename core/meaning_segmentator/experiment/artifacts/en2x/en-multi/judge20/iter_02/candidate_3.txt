[Core Principles]
- Does the material after this cut overturn, reverse, or negate the claim made by the stretch before it? In English this often appears via post-auxiliary negation, concessives, or contrastive continuations; such reversals make a cut harmful.
- Does what follows supply the predicate, object, or core complement needed to complete the already-heard SVO proposition? Cutting before that material leaves an orphan statement that will mistranslate.
- Do the next one to four words form a short prepositional, temporal, locative, or measurement complement that is essential to the verb or copula? In English these short complements commonly complete the predicate and must be kept attached.
- Does the continuation resolve how a clause-initial temporal or locative adverbial attaches? If attachment is still ambiguous, separating it can change the asserted time/place relation.
- Does the continuation determine the scope of a scope-limiting particle, approximate numeral, conditional introducer, or modal? If scope is undecided until later words, splitting will misrepresent quantification or conditionality.
- Does the continuation complete a phrasal verb or verb+particle sequence whose combined semantics form the predicate? Splitting verb and particle or separated multiword predicate parts damages meaning.
- Does the continuation supply essential post-nominal identification, restriction, apposition, title, or possessive relation that identifies or assigns a role to a previously heard noun? Cutting before it risks misidentification or omission.
- For each candidate cut, ask two measurement questions: will translating left and right separately and then joining still convey the whole sentence (cohesion)? And does the whole sentence, when read, contradict the left fragment (contra)?
