[Core Principles]
- Does what follows the marker overturn or contradict the truth of what preceded it? An explicit sentential negation or an adversative subordinate that flips the proposition is the most severe; a later narrow modifier that only refines scope is the mildest.
- Does translating the two sides separately fail to compose the whole sentence's predication or argument structure? If the following tokens supply an obligatory object or clausal complement needed to complete the main verb, the damage is severe; if they only add optional elaboration or exemplification, it is milder.
- Does the continuation introduce a subordinate clause that restricts or supplies essential information for a preceding noun or verb? An essential relative or complement that changes who/what is referenced is severe; a clearly nonrestrictive parenthetical is milder.
- Does the text after the marker begin a clause-level discourse relation (contrast, cause, condition) that changes the main relation of the sentence? If it flips polarity or changes whether an action occurs, that is severe; if it merely sequences or shades information, that is milder.
- Is the marker located inside an ongoing noun phrase ( modifiers, appositive, or prepositional complement) that attaches to a head? Splitting a head from its obligatory complement is severe; separating off extra descriptive modifiers or detachable appositives is milder.
- Is the marker between a verb and an argument (object or sentential complement) leaving the verb without its required complement? A transitive verb separated from its required object or a verb missing its clausal complement is severe; when following material is an optional adjunct, it is milder.
- Is the marker inside or immediately after initial adjuncts (temporal/locative prepositional phrases, participial modifiers) whose scope could be revised by what follows? It is worst where later material clearly reattaches the adjunct to a different head; it is mild when the adjunct is clearly detachable background information.
- Is the marker inside a coordinate or list sequence where items are parallel and individually interpretable? Cuts here are mild when each coordinate is an independent item; they are worse when the coordinator plus the next item jointly express a meaning that a single item does not.

[Order Principles]
- When one marker falls between a verb and its essential object/complement and another falls earlier after an initial adjunct that may reattach, prefer the verb-object cut only if the adjunct cannot be interpreted independently; otherwise prefer the earlier adjunct cut.
- When one marker splits a noun from an essential relative/complement and another splits off a nonrestrictive parenthetical later in the same noun phrase, prefer the parenthetical cut.
- When two markers are both inside a coordinate list but one separates the coordinator from its following item while the other separates two items later in the list, prefer cutting later in the list unless the earlier cut isolates an item that is independently grammatical and self-contained.
