Cutting before a coordinating contrast that changes truth conditions is severe; cutting before a simple additive continuation is milder.

- Does separating here materially reduce the cohesion of translation across typological gaps (word order change, required connective insertion in target languages)? Splits that force reordering of core relations or require inferred connectives are most severe; splits that only drop optional linking words are milder.

[Order Principles]
- When one marker would separate a head noun from a following relative or essential complement and another marker lies earlier inside a preceding adjunct, prefer the later marker that keeps the noun with its relative/essential complement.

- When one marker would split a verb from its primary object and an alternative marker splits an optional adjunct earlier in the clause, prefer the alternative that preserves the verb–object relation.
